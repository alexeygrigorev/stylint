# One More Call for Buildcamp

The AI Engineering Buildcamp runs for nine weeks, during which participants build a project that grows throughout the program. Registration for the cohort described in the announcement is open until April 13.

After that, I plan to pause the course and focus on AI Shipping Labs, so I wouldn't assume another cohort will happen soon.

The course has one running example, the Documentation Agent. Participants also see an FAQ assistant, a YouTube transcript summarizer, and a PDF book processor for RAG.

The optional projects continue with a web search agent, YouTube researcher, and coding agent. They also include a code analysis agent and a deep research agent, showing how patterns change with the data and product.

Each week also ends with homework. The first two weeks cover document processing and a RAG pipeline built from books.

Week 3 is a Wikipedia Agent. Week 4 uses a DuckDB SQL Agent over New York City taxi data, with pytest tests, LLM judges, and cost tracking.

Week 5 builds a Trivia Quizmaster instrumented with Logfire. Week 6 focuses on systematic evaluation through a Recipe Assistant, including more than 20 evaluation scenarios and hallucination detection.

The capstone starts immediately, with participants defining a use case and building the first RAG version during weeks 1 and 2.

Week 3 adds tools and makes it agentic. Week 4 adds testing, week 5 adds monitoring and logs, and week 6 evaluates interactions and generated scenarios.

Weeks 7 and 8 are for polishing, extending, and deploying. In week 9, participants present the result and receive peer feedback.

I changed the project-definition part because the first two cohorts showed a recurring problem. Some participants spent too long deciding what to build and then didn't finish the capstone. The third cohort uses a design-thinking framework with prompts and templates.

If you already have an idea, it helps structure and scope it. If you don't, it gives you a way to start immediately.

The target is to have a working capstone by the end of week 6, leaving the final weeks for refinement and presentation.

The workload is designed for working professionals, but it's real work. Most activities are asynchronous, with lessons, exercises, and homework taking roughly 3–10 hours per week depending on background and pace.

There's one live office-hours session each week, which I record and share with a written summary.

The standard price is $1,799. Company learning budgets and team discounts are available, along with a 20% Substack promo code.

Scholarship selection for this cohort is already closed, although I provide full and partial scholarships for new cohorts. Student discount and PPP questions can be sent to me directly.

The course is for building and debugging rather than passively watching lessons. The sequence covers RAG, agents, and testing. It then adds observability, evaluation, and deployment, leaving participants with a project they can explain and continue developing.
