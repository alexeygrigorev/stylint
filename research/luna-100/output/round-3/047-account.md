# Starting AI Shipping Labs

Valeriia and I are launching AI Shipping Labs, a community for people who want to build and ship AI products. We have worked together on DataTalks.Club for a long time, but this is a new project with a different purpose. The launch event is scheduled for April 13.

The idea came from a problem I kept seeing. AI changes quickly, so people collect frameworks and tools without a clear plan.

Someone has a full-time job and keeps pushing an AI project to next weekend. Someone else wants to move into a new role and doesn't know what to learn first. Tutorials can leave you with a prototype that breaks as soon as you try to build something independently.

AI Shipping Labs is meant to add structure around that work. Members get a Slack workspace and personal onboarding while the community is small. They also get a plan based on where they're now and where they want to go.

Members join accountability circles and live building sessions. They can also use group learning, career support, private discussions, and specialized mini-courses.

Accountability circles are the part I expect to matter most. I get requests for mentoring, but I usually don't have the bandwidth for one-on-one work, and it doesn't scale. Many people need a plan, a deadline, and a reason to arrive prepared more than they need a personal mentor.

The circles run in sprints with a clear start and end. Members at a similar level share what they've done and what they plan to do. They also discuss what's blocking them and help each other work through those blockers.

Building sessions happen once or twice a month and last 1.5 to 2 hours. Sometimes I bring a problem, and sometimes a member brings one. We work on the problem instead of watching slides. In group learning, members choose a topic, research it, and present what they found. A cohort makes it easier to keep going through a course or new material.

The private setting also changes the conversations. Members can ask for resume and LinkedIn feedback, discuss personal branding, and talk about learning in public. They can also discuss interview questions, salary negotiations, or whether it's time to leave a job without putting those conversations on a public timeline.

We have ideas for short courses on Python for AI Engineering, Spec-Driven Development for AI, and refactoring AI-generated code. Early members can vote on what gets built next. They also receive access to planned April and May events. These include a FastAPI and vector database workshop, a LinkedIn webinar led by Valeriia, an interactive coding session, and a live take-home assignment workshop.

The platform is brand new, so some signup automation may fail. The current site is still the Next.js version Valeriia built while I continue the new version. The larger platform will add authentication and access levels. It will also include a course catalog, progress tracking, a calendar, and voting.

DataTalks.Club isn't changing: its free Zoomcamps continue. AI Shipping Labs grew from the Buildcamp, this newsletter, and the desire for a smaller group that could solve problems together.
