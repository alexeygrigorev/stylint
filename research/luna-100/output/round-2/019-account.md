# Researching what companies mean by AI Engineer

I've been seeing AI Engineer and Gen AI Engineer more often on job boards and LinkedIn, as well as in community discussions. The titles are becoming familiar, but the work depends on the company and team. Expectations can differ enough to contradict one another.

The question matters to me because I run AI Engineering Buildcamp. During Office Hours, a participant asked for real examples of take-home assignments used in AI Engineer interviews. I wanted data I could examine before sharing an answer.

I've worked with ML and AI for more than 15 years and regularly talk to people doing this kind of work. But I'm not currently employed as an AI Engineer. I didn't want to base the research only on my experience or on what I heard in conversations.

I started with my own view of the role, including how the day is organized and which problems an AI Engineer works on. I'll present that perspective before comparing it with evidence from the market.

For the employer side, I collected more than 1,500 job postings from the previous three months. They cover cities such as Los Angeles and New York, along with London, Amsterdam, and Berlin.

Some of those jobs have different titles while still involving AI or generative AI work. I want to compare the names and responsibilities, then look at the skills and tools companies ask for. I'm also interested in the overlap with ML Engineer positions.

I looked through interview stories on Twitter/X and Reddit. People share questions they were asked and describe the work they do. Research agents helped with this collection, including Grok, which analyzed X posts to find recent interview discussions.

I also plan to talk directly with people who hire AI Engineers. Other conversations will include DataTalks.Club members who are interviewing or already working in the role, plus engineers I connect with through LinkedIn.

Some discussions may become podcast episodes. Others will stay private but still inform the analysis of interview structure and hiring decisions. I want to understand how people are evaluated, alongside what employers put in a job description.

For take-home work, I collected around 60 public GitHub repositories containing submitted solutions. I tried deep research agents from Google and OpenAI, but they didn't reliably find the 100 repositories I requested. Sometimes they found a real repository and then invented additional results.

They did help me write better search queries. I used those queries to find repositories, then reviewed and aggregated the results manually for local analysis.

I'll share the research in four live sessions, starting with my view of an AI Engineer's day and then the job-posting data. The remaining sessions cover interviews and take-home assignments.
