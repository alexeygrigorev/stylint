# Forward-Deployed Engineers in Practice

In 2026, forward-deployed engineering is a growing part of the AI engineering market. Since January, I have scraped AI Engineering job descriptions for the AI Engineering Field Guide. The dataset has 4,894 descriptions. In my dataset, FDE-related postings grew from 28 in January to 108 in July. The wider AI Engineering market doubled over the same six months.

I analyzed 113 FDE postings from January through July. The dataset focuses on AI Engineering roles, so its picture of the market has that limitation. It still shows a role that sits between software engineering and the people using the software.

Palantir created the forward-deployed engineering model in 2006. It had a general platform, but making it useful required adapting it to each customer's data, workflows, and infrastructure. Engineers worked inside customer teams, built integrations and custom components, and sent recurring needs back to the product.

The same gap appears in enterprise AI. A general-purpose model needs customer data, systems, and workflows before it can deliver value in production. Many organizations don't have the engineering capacity or product knowledge to close that gap themselves. An FDE works alongside the customer to define the problem and adapt the product. They build missing software, deploy and debug the system, and feed recurring needs back to the core product.

The postings give a breakdown of the responsibilities. Direct client engagement appeared in 90 percent of the descriptions, and building and deploying production systems appeared in 87 percent. System, API, and data integration appeared in 62 percent.

Scoping and requirements gathering appeared in 51 percent, while testing, evaluation, and monitoring appeared in 41 percent. Thirty-nine percent mentioned bringing customer feedback back to the product roadmap. Twenty-five percent mentioned prototypes or proofs of concept.

FDE work starts with a business outcome. The engineer learns the existing workflow, examines available data, and identifies constraints before turning that information into requirements and success criteria.

Once the problem is clear, they handle ordinary production work. That includes authentication and permissions. It also includes testing and error handling. Deployment, logging, and observability follow.

Integration is a large part of the role. An FDE may build connectors and data pipelines, then transform data and configure access controls. They connect APIs and deploy inside the customer's cloud.

After deployment the engineer uses evaluation criteria, logs, traces, and metrics to find failures. User feedback also helps find failures. Some problems belong only to one customer. Others reveal a reusable product need.

The role overlaps with consulting, solutions engineering, and AI engineering, but the production responsibility is different. An FDE writes production code, owns deployment, and stays involved until the system works for a particular customer. The role requires broad engineering skills along with customer discovery, communication, and judgment about scope.
