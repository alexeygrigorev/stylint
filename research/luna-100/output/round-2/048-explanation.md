# A Structure for Learning and Shipping AI Projects

Learning AI alone often fails for practical reasons. You may have a full-time job and keep moving your project to next weekend. You may want a new role and have no idea which tools to learn first. Or you may follow a tutorial, build a prototype, and then get stuck when it breaks outside the tutorial.

The problem isn't always a lack of resources. There are too many resources, and they don't provide a sequence or a reason to return tomorrow. A useful community adds those missing pieces through a plan, regular check-ins, and people working on similar problems.

AI Shipping Labs is designed around that structure. Members get access to Slack and personal onboarding while the community is small. The onboarding can be a short call, a voice-message exchange, or an asynchronous conversation. The purpose is to understand where you're now and where you want to go, then turn that into a plan.

Accountability circles are small mastermind groups that work in sprints. A sprint has a start and an end, and members at similar levels share progress on a deadline. The format resembles a company standup. You explain what you did, what you plan to do, and what's blocking you. The group can then help with the blocker instead of leaving the problem private and vague.

This structure is different from one-on-one mentoring. I often decline mentoring requests because I don't have the bandwidth, and individual mentoring doesn't scale. In many cases, the missing ingredient is a deadline and a reason to arrive prepared. The circle supplies those without requiring one person to solve every member's problem.

Building sessions add direct practice. They happen once or twice a month for 1.5 to 2 hours. I bring a real problem, or a member brings one, and the group works on it together instead of watching slides.

Group learning uses a similar structure. Members choose a topic, research it, and present what they found. A cohort makes it easier to continue through a course or new material.

The private community also gives career conversations a place to happen. Members can get resume and LinkedIn reviews. They can discuss personal branding and learning in public. Interview expectations, salary negotiations, and changing jobs can be difficult to discuss on a public timeline.

Short specialized courses are planned as well. The initial ideas include Python for AI Engineering and Spec-Driven Development for AI. Refactoring AI Slop is another idea. Early members can vote on which courses to develop next, so the catalog depends on what the community actually needs.

The model is therefore a loop. Choose a concrete project, make a plan, show up with progress, and use other people's experience when the work gets stuck. It doesn't promise that every project will succeed. It gives the project a place, a deadline, and a group that can notice when progress has stopped.
