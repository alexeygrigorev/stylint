# A Five-Part Research Plan for AI Engineering

The title AI Engineer is still forming, so a useful definition needs more than one source. Companies describe responsibilities one way, candidates show what they can build, and interviews reveal how hiring decisions work. My research plan combines these perspectives instead of treating a job title as a fixed description.

## Start with a working perspective

The first source is my own industry experience. I have worked with ML and AI for more than 15 years, and I teach the AI Engineering Buildcamp. That gives me a structured view of the problems, skills and daily work an AI Engineer might have. I plan to present that perspective first, then compare it with the market rather than treating it as the final answer.

## Collect the demand side

The second source is a dataset of more than 1,500 job postings from the previous three months. The postings cover Los Angeles, New York, London, Amsterdam, Berlin and other cities. Some use titles other than AI Engineer even though they involve AI or generative AI work.

That variation is useful evidence. The dataset can show which responsibilities companies assign, which skills recur and where AI Engineer work overlaps with ML Engineer roles. It can also separate naming habits from the work people actually do.

## Look at interview evidence

The third source is made of interview stories from Twitter or X and Reddit. Engineers often share the questions they received and describe their work after an interview. Research agents helped collect these discussions, including an experiment where Grok analyzed X posts. This material is informal, but it can reveal recent interview patterns that are absent from official job descriptions.

The fourth source is direct conversations with people in the field. I planned discussions with hiring managers, DataTalksClub Slack members who were interviewing or working as AI Engineers, and engineers I met through LinkedIn. Some conversations may become public podcast episodes. Others can remain private while still contributing to the analysis.

## Study take-home assignments

The fifth source is a collection of around 60 public GitHub repositories containing take-home assignment solutions. These repositories show what candidates were asked to build and what they chose to submit.

I also tested deep research agents from Google and OpenAI while searching for repositories. They did not reliably find 100 repositories as requested, and sometimes they returned one real result followed by hallucinated additions. Their useful role was narrower: they helped produce better GitHub search queries. I manually reviewed and aggregated the repositories for local analysis.

Together, these sources cover experience, demand, interviews, conversations and candidate artifacts. The comparison matters because the AI Engineer role is being shaped by both what companies expect and what candidates can deliver. The resulting definition should remain a working view that can change as the market changes.
