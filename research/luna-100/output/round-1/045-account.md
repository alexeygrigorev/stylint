# The next AI Engineering Buildcamp

On April 13 I planned to start another iteration of the AI Engineering Buildcamp. I also decided that I would pause the course afterward and focus on AI Shipping Labs for a while. That made this cohort different from a routine enrollment announcement. People considering the course needed to know both what they would build and that another cohort might not happen soon.

The course is built around learning by building. The running example is a Documentation Agent over Evidently documentation. In week one, participants learn the OpenAI API, RAG, and search and produce a working question-answering system. Later weeks add agentic behavior, testing, monitoring, and systematic evaluation. By week six, the example has moved from retrieval to a tool-using agent that is tested, observable, and evaluated with human and synthetic data.

The running example is only one path through the material. Optional projects include an FAQ Assistant, a YouTube Transcript Summarizer, and a PDF Book Processor. The agent examples include web search, YouTube research, coding, code analysis, and deep research. These projects show how the same patterns change when the data and product are different.

Each week also has a homework project. Participants process documents and build a RAG pipeline, create a Wikipedia Agent, test a DuckDB SQL Agent over New York taxi data, instrument a Trivia Quizmaster, and evaluate a Recipe Assistant with more than 20 scenarios. The capstone grows alongside this work. Weeks one and two establish the use case and first RAG version, week three adds tools, week four adds tests, and week five adds monitoring. Evaluation follows, then polishing, deployment, and presentation.

I had noticed in the first two cohorts that some people spent too long deciding what to build and then did not finish the capstone. For the third cohort I added a design-thinking methodology for the project-definition stage. People with an idea can scope it, while people without one get prompts and templates. The intended result is a working capstone by the end of week six, leaving later weeks for refinement.

The course lasts about nine weeks and is designed for working professionals. Most work is asynchronous, with three to ten hours per week depending on background and pace. There is one live office-hours session each week, recorded with a written summary.

The format is intentionally active. The lessons and examples provide a path, but participants are expected to build, debug, and make decisions about their own project. The weekly office hours are for technical questions, architecture decisions, and feedback rather than replacing that work.

The standard price was $1,799. Company learning budgets, team discounts, scholarships, and a newsletter discount were available, although the scholarship form for this cohort had already closed. Registration was open until April 13. After that date, I expected to pause the course while working on AI Shipping Labs.
