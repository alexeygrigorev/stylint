# Starting AI Shipping Labs

Valeriia and I launched AI Shipping Labs as a community for people who want to build AI products and ship them. We had worked together on DataTalks.Club for a long time, but this was a different kind of project. The launch included a live event on April 13 and an invitation to people who wanted structure around learning and building.

The idea came from a problem I kept seeing. AI changes quickly, and people collect new tools and frameworks without a clear direction. Someone with a full-time job keeps moving a project to next weekend. Someone trying to change roles does not know what to learn first. Someone follows tutorials but still cannot build alone, or creates a prototype and cannot repair it when it breaks. In each case, the person is learning without enough structure or support.

AI Shipping Labs is intended to provide that structure. Members get a Slack workspace and, while the community is small, personal onboarding from me through a short call, voice messages, or an asynchronous conversation. We start by understanding where someone is and where they want to go, then use the community activities to keep moving.

Accountability circles are mastermind groups that work in sprints. Participants at a similar level share what they did, what they plan to do, and what is blocking them. I used to decline most mentoring requests because one-on-one mentoring does not scale. The circle format focuses on a plan, a deadline, and showing up prepared.

Building sessions happen once or twice a month for 1.5 to 2 hours. Sometimes I bring a problem and sometimes a member does. Group learning follows a similar idea: members choose a topic, research it, and present what they found. The group makes it easier to stay on track than learning alone.

The community also includes career support. Members can get feedback on resumes and LinkedIn profiles, discuss learning in public and personal branding, and talk privately about interviews, offers, salary negotiation, or career changes. Planned mini-courses cover specialized topics, with members voting on what to develop. Ideas include Python for AI Engineering, Spec-Driven Development for AI, and refactoring hard-to-maintain AI-generated code.

Early members also had access to April and May events: a FastAPI and vector database workshop, a LinkedIn webinar led by Valeriia, an interactive coding session, and a live take-home assignment workshop. The website was still new, so some sign-up automations might fail and required manual help.

The platform was being built in parallel. Planned features included authentication, access levels, a course catalog, progress tracking, a calendar, and voting. The existing Next.js site remained in use while the new version was developed. AI Shipping Labs did not replace DataTalks.Club or its free Zoomcamps. It was a smaller, paid community shaped by the demand for ongoing practical support.
